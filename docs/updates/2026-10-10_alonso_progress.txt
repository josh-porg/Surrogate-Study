PROGRESS REPORT: SURROGATE-MODELLING PAPER (for an update to Prof. J. J. Alonso)
Source material for drafting an email. Status as of 10 October 2026. All findings are preliminary unless stated.

CONTEXT
- The paper extends the surrogate-model study in my M.S. thesis (Poznanski, Univ. of Kansas, 2026; Sec. 14.3 and
  App. G).
- It asks a question the thesis did not: does the most accurate surrogate also give the best design once an
  optimizer runs on it? The primary metric is true regret at matched computational cost, not prediction error.
- Two fixes to the thesis methodology are built in. Surrogates are scored against clean truth instead of noisy
  cross-validation folds, and the hypotheses were pre-registered before the main experiment ran. Any deviation is
  logged.

COMPLETED
- E0 (cost model): timing of every learner, used to budget the study.
- E1 (reproduction gate): 71 of 72 published thesis results were reproduced within confidence intervals using the
  original thesis code.
  - Scored against clean truth, the true errors are 2-4x smaller than the published noisy-CV numbers. The rankings
    barely change (Spearman 0.91-0.99).
  - The 3% noise floor had compressed the gaps between methods. On the smooth surface, the GP's lead over GBT is
    2.4x on clean truth versus 1.2x as published.
  - So the thesis conclusion stands and is stronger, but the published magnitudes were dominated by noise.
- E2a (learner screening): 32,300 fits, covering 45 learner configurations from GPs to tree ensembles, neural nets,
  KANs and sparse-basis methods.
  - The GP family led overall, and each method won where its design predicts (e.g. trees on step functions).
  - One finding matters for the study itself: the optimizer used to train a neural network can matter more than the
    network. Full-batch L-BFGS beat Adam by an order of magnitude at engineering sample sizes.
  - Screening produced the 35-configuration shortlist for the main experiment.

IN PROGRESS
- E3 (main learner study): 826,020 surrogate fits in total, covering 35 learners, 82 test problems, 6 sample sizes,
  11 noise conditions and 10 seeds. About 66% are done (541,000 fits).
- Early E3 signals, from the slower learners only; the GP and boosted-tree baselines are still running:
  - Neural-network ensembles trained with L-BFGS rank first in most problem classes at n = 600-1000.
  - They also give the best gradient accuracy, which matters for gradient-based design optimization.
  - Calibration differs widely between methods: some intervals are too wide, others too narrow.
  - The central comparison (GP vs boosted trees vs networks, measured by design regret) is not in yet.
- One logged deviation: SMT kriging was capped at n <= 500 because every fit at n = 1000 exceeded the 15-minute
  per-fit limit.

WHAT IS SLOWING PROGRESS: LOSS OF COMPUTE AFTER GRADUATION
- During the thesis I used university computing. After graduating, the study runs on a personal laptop (12 cores,
  16 GB RAM) plus Kaggle's free CPU tier: 5 concurrent sessions of 4 cores, each capped at about 12 hours.
- That is about 20 cores in 10.5-hour waves. Each wave has to be launched, collected and backed up by hand-built
  tooling.
- In practice:
  - E3 alone takes about a week of continuous waves. On a cluster allocation it would take a few days.
  - The most expensive learners (exact GPs and kriging at large n) had to be capped or scheduled around memory and
    time limits.
  - Engineering effort went into reliability: resumable runs, verified downloads, off-laptop backups.
- The remaining experiments (E4 design-optimization regret, E5-E10, and the real FEA data in E11) face the same
  limits. The estimated total is about 2-3 weeks of compute.
- An allocation would cut that to days. Options include an NSF ACCESS Explore allocation, or access through a lab.

NEXT STEPS
- Finish E3: about 3 more days for the main learners, then about 1.5 days for SMT kriging.
- E4: run the surviving surrogates inside design optimizers and measure true regret at matched cost. This is the
  paper's central claim.
- E5-E10: sampling strategies, multi-fidelity surrogates, upscaling tree models for smooth gradients, and others.
- E11: the real thermo-mechanical FEA dataset from the thesis (623 usable runs, about 1 CPU-hour each). This stays
  on my own machine.
- Target: a full draft once E4 is in. I would welcome your views on the framing (accuracy vs design regret) and
  on venue.
