\section{Changing the domain: latent, sparse-basis and low-rank surrogates}
\label{sec:latent}

Data-driven reduced-order modelling succeeds by finding a basis in which dynamics are simple: proper orthogonal
decomposition and dynamic mode decomposition find low-rank linear structure, SINDy finds a sparse dictionary
representation \cite{brunton2016discovering}, and the SINDy autoencoder learns the coordinates and the sparse model
jointly \cite{champion2019data}. Compressed and randomised DMD do not remove any stage; they compute the same
decomposition from a sketch of the data, with cost set by the intrinsic rank rather than the ambient size
\cite{erichson2019compressed,erichson2019randomized,halko2011finding}. The same ideas apply to static response surfaces,
where they are scattered across communities that rarely cite each other.

\paragraph{Linear input reduction.} Projection pursuit regression sums smooth functions of learned projections
\cite{friedman1981projection}; active subspaces take the dominant eigenvectors of the expected gradient outer product
\cite{constantine2015active}; polynomial ridge approximation fits the subspace and the polynomial jointly without
gradients \cite{hokanson2018data}; GPs with built-in dimension reduction learn the projection inside the kernel
\cite{tripathy2016gaussian}; random embeddings exploit low intrinsic dimension for optimization \cite{wang2016bayesian}.

\paragraph{Co-learned nonlinear maps.} Deep kernel learning places a GP on the output of a neural feature map and trains
both by marginal likelihood \cite{wilson2016deep}---the most direct form of ``learning the domain and the fit
together''. Its weakness is decisive at engineering sample sizes: with many hyperparameters the marginal likelihood
prefers to correlate all points, and the method can over-fit worse than a plain network unless treated fully
Bayesianly \cite{ober2021promises}. Kernel flows learn kernels by a cross-validation-like criterion instead
\cite{owhadi2019kernel}; input warping restricts the map to per-axis monotone transforms \cite{snoek2014input};
Fourier features and spectral-mixture kernels learn spectral content
\cite{tancik2020fourier,wilson2013gaussian}. Latent-space optimization with variational autoencoders works for
structured inputs \cite{gomezbombarelli2018automatic}, but an autoencoder trained only on uniformly sampled inputs learns
the sampling density, not the response; for static surrogates the map must be supervised.

\paragraph{Sparse bases and compressed sensing.} If the response is sparse in a known basis, $\ell_1$ recovery needs
samples in proportion to the sparsity rather than the basis size \cite{candes2006robust,donoho2006compressed}; for
orthogonal polynomial bases this yields compressive-sampling polynomial chaos
\cite{rauhut2012sparse,doostan2011non,hampton2015compressive}, and wavelets do the same for localised features
\cite{donoho1994ideal}. The guarantees need random samples from the right measure, which couples the basis to the
sampling design.

\paragraph{Low rank: the singular value decomposition of a surface.} A bivariate function admits a Schmidt (functional
SVD) expansion, the basis of two-dimensional Chebyshev technology \cite{townsend2013extension}; in higher dimension the
tensor-train format stores a function with cost linear in dimension and learns it by cross approximation from adaptively
chosen samples \cite{oseledets2011tensor,oseledets2010tt}, with continuous analogues for surrogates
\cite{gorodetsky2019continuous}. Randomised Tucker and tensor-train decompositions compress structured grids
\cite{minster2020randomized}; Nystr\"om and random-feature approximations compress kernel matrices
\cite{williams2001using,rahimi2007random}; sketching compresses least squares \cite{woodruff2014sketching}. These are the
static counterparts of compressed DMD: they accelerate an intermediate stage---for us, densification of the
intermediate model onto a grid, and repeated refits of the scout---without changing what is computed.

\paragraph{Automatic co-search.} Choosing a transform, a basis and a learner together is an instance of the combined
algorithm selection and hyperparameter optimization problem of automated machine learning
\cite{thornton2013auto,feurer2015efficient}; its selection bias must be controlled by nested validation.
